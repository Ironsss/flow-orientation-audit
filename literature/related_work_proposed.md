## 2.1 Directional and graph-based features

Directional imbalance is an old and interpretable detection primitive. MULTOPS flags bandwidth attacks from a disproportion between the packet rates to and from a prefix, on the premise that the two rates are proportional in normal operation (Gil & Poletto 2001)†. Scan detection rests on the fraction of connection attempts that receive a reply (Jung et al. 2004)†. The Producer–Consumer Ratio normalises sent minus received bytes by their sum, so that it is independent of load (Bullard & Gerth 2014, presented at FloCon; not peer reviewed)†. Flow-based intrusion detection is surveyed by Sperotto et al. (2010)†, and bidirectional flow export gives the forward and reverse counters these features use; RFC 5103 (Trammell & Boschi 2008)† lists the separation of answered and unanswered requests among its uses. Graph-based methods aggregate relational structure over hosts (Akoglu et al. 2015; Zhong et al. 2024)†. These works propose or apply directional or topological signals inside one setting. Whether the *sign* of such a signal is stable across datasets is a different question, and it is the one studied here.

## 2.2 Cross-dataset generalisation of learning-based NIDS

Closed-world benchmark results can overstate field performance (Sommer & Paxson 2010), and pitfalls in evaluating learning-based security systems are widespread (Arp et al. 2022). Cross-dataset studies of flow classifiers report the same picture: strong inter-dataset generalisation is not implied by strong intra-dataset scores (D'hooge et al. 2020)†, cross-dataset accuracy is close to chance except for a few attack–dataset pairs (Cantone et al. 2024), and no model generalises across all of four NetFlow-v2 datasets (Layeghy & Portmann 2023). The last is the closest prior observation to ours. Its SHAP summary plots show that, for a given feature, the correspondence between low or high values and the Benign or Attack class present in one target domain is absent in another (Layeghy & Portmann 2023, arXiv preprint). The evidence is model-level and concerns features chosen by the model, not the sign of one fixed feature. A distinct strand studies score orientation: under HIKARI-2021→CICIDS2017 transfer some detectors show an inverted score orientation (AUC below 0.5), and an oracle-flipped AUC, max{AUC, 1−AUC}, is used as a diagnostic (Jin & Lee 2026)†. Comparable score-direction instability is reported for image anomaly detection (Ascarate et al. 2026)†. Both concern the scores of trained detectors, not a fixed interpretable feature evaluated against named reference populations.

## 2.3 Dataset, labelling and exporter artifacts

The benchmarks used here differ in testbed, generator and exporter (Sharafaldin et al. 2018; Moustafa & Slay 2015; Koroniotis et al. 2019). Flow construction and labelling errors have been documented for CICIDS2017 and CSE-CIC-IDS-2018, including flows assigned the wrong direction with source and destination swapped after a TCP timeout, and many "attack" flows without forward data (Engelen et al. 2021), with corrected recreations (Liu et al. 2022†; Lanvin et al. 2023†). An analysis of seven benchmark datasets identifies design smells, among them features of outsized importance unrelated to the attack mechanism (Flood et al. 2024), and a single TTL feature nearly matches the full feature set on UNSW-NB15, which its authors attribute to the testbed design (Sarhan et al. 2021, feature analysis). Synthetic benign traffic differs statistically from real benign traffic for most features (Layeghy et al. 2024)†, and the benign class has internal structure (Wilkinson & Thomson 2025)†. Re-exporting the same PCAP with different exporters changes detection results (Pinto et al. 2025), and a NetFlow can be seen as a function of the communication, the network and the exporter configuration (Apruzzese et al. 2022). Common NetFlow re-exports make feature sets comparable across benchmarks (Sarhan et al. 2021, NetFlow datasets†; 2022). These studies motivate treating a dataset as a measurement instrument. None reports how such choices affect the sign of a single directional feature.

## 2.4 Orientation versus separability as a measurement issue

For a score, the sign of its association with the label and the strength of that association are different quantities: replacing the score by its negation maps AUC to 1−AUC and leaves max{AUC, 1−AUC} unchanged. This is why the oracle-flipped AUC is a common diagnostic (Jin & Lee 2026)†. In this paper the distinction is applied to one fixed feature, with the benign reference stated in every number, so that a change of sign across references, reply slices, dependence structures or representations can be reported next to the magnitude of separation.

## 2.5 Preregistration, dependence and reproducibility

Committing to a plan before analysis limits analytic flexibility (Nosek et al. 2018), and data leakage and other errors undermine ML-based findings (Kapoor & Narayanan 2023)†. We use a freeze-then-run protocol to separate frozen verdicts from later audits; we do not claim the protocol as new. Flows are nested in host pairs, and cluster-level resampling is the consistent alternative (Field & Welsh 2007)†; with few clusters standard cluster-robust inference over-rejects (Cameron, Gelbach & Miller 2008)†. Sampling changes the metrics available to anomaly detectors (Brauckhoff et al. 2006†; Mai et al. 2006†), which frames the information-restriction question (H2).

## 2.6 Positioning

Across the sources examined, classifier performance, model-level attributions and detector score orientation are shown not to transport between NIDS datasets, and dataset and exporter artifacts are documented. The contribution of this paper is narrower: an audited measurement of the sign of one fixed, interpretable directional feature against named benign references, with preregistered external tests and reported negative results. A systematic search for prior work on feature-sign instability was not part of this study, so we make no claim of priority.
